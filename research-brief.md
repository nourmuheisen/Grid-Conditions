# Grid / Conditions — a national evidence screen

**Independent portfolio research by Nour Abu Muheisen · Version 2 · 15 October 2026**

## Decision and finding

For publicly documented data-centre locations in the Netherlands, where might flexible access, workload shifting or on-site supply warrant investigation, and what would need to be established before choosing an option?

**Public evidence supports identifying investigation questions, not ranking actual grid benefit or connection feasibility.** The expanded screen covers 12 real cases across eight provinces and six operators. It includes operating locations, announced expansion and phased delivery. These are not 12 unconnected projects, and the sample is not a national census.

BCG's August 2026 Northwest Europe perspective supplies context for considering connection arrangements, workload flexibility and on-site assets together [S01]. This project does not copy its model or numerical estimates. BCG did not commission, endorse or contribute to it. No DAMEN information was used.

## National scope and selection

The first version tested the method in Groningen. The national extension retains those four cases and adds documented locations in Noord-Holland, Zuid-Holland, Noord-Brabant, Flevoland, Utrecht, Gelderland and Overijssel. Eight provinces are represented; missing provinces are not assumed to lack data centres.

DDA's map is a useful discovery resource, not a verified project pipeline [S02]. RVO reporting obligations do not themselves constitute an accessible register of site connection contracts [S03]. Owner directories and announcements identify cases; official notices and grid-operator publications provide separate evidence layers. This review does not claim that a complete national open register is impossible, only that one was not established here.

Inclusion required an identifiable owner source and a specific investigative question. The selection favours variation in geography, project stage and evidence type, not capacity or apparent attractiveness. Large operators with accessible disclosures remain overrepresented: five cases are NorthC and three are Google. The units differ—individual facilities, campus locations and two-location aggregates—so counts cannot be read as a market inventory and capacities are not summed. `selection-register.csv` records included cases and two considered but unselected alternatives.

## What can be compared responsibly?

| Dimension | Public evidence supports | It does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Grid | Regional constraints; selected planned connections and owner voltage specifications | Contracted MW, spare capacity, queue position or an alternative offer |
| Flexibility | Operator precedent and selected service obligations | Site-controllable workload, notice tolerance, event duration or rebound |
| On-site assets | Selected backup/solar specifications and announced designs | Dependable routine output, fuel availability or dispatch permission |
| Water/local context | Some cooling descriptions and proposed heat uses | Measured water risk, drought rights or net system benefit |
| Delivery | Dated announcements, official notices and phase descriptions | Complete current commissioning status or a credible time-to-power counterfactual |

## Findings strengthened by the national extension

**Phase boundaries matter.** AMS11's owner page says operational in 2026, while industry reporting describes an initial opening in November 2025 [S25, S26]. A phased build or stale page may explain this; the tool retains both. Its reported IT capacity is not converted to total electrical demand or treated as fully energised capacity.

**Resilience and flexibility are different questions.** BIT-1's service terms provide a concrete starting point for discussing power obligations [S33]. NorthC Eindhoven 2's hybrid backup disclosure motivates a permission-and-reserve investigation [S28, S29]. Neither supplies a verified routine load-reduction commitment.

**Geography is not topology.** Stedin's port-area congestion account is not attributed to Zestienhoven [S38]. Regional reinforcement delays in the Flevopolder, Gelderland and Utrecht do not establish individual connection waits [S36]. Enexis's Bolderhoek findings are not assigned to PDC2 without a connection match [S39].

## Discrepancies, freshness and interpretation

The claim ledger preserves the Winschoten 2023/2024 groundbreaking discrepancy [S04–S06], revised expectations in Eemshaven cable reporting [S21, S22], and the unnamed operator in a 380 kV proposal [S15]. The latter is not merged with the reported 220 kV Google project. Bolderhoek's older 2027 works expectation and revised end-2028 account are distinguished [S39]; neither becomes a PDC2 delivery date.

“Verified source statement” means the source makes the statement; it is not an independent asset audit. An owner page without a date remains undated. Access dates, publication dates and forecast milestone dates are separate. Unknown means not established in the reviewed set, not zero or absent. Import and export constraints remain distinct.

## Prototype, limits and consulting next steps

The redesigned prototype offers search, province and investigation-theme filters, claim-level provenance, a comparison of up to three cases, and three qualitative scenario pathways. Editable notes are always labelled user assumptions. No numerical feasibility, savings, emissions or time-to-power result is calculated.

A later model would need load and curtailment time series, tenant consent, usable asset power/energy/fuel, reserve policy, permits and comparable commercial offers. System benefit also requires a location-specific counterfactual and rebound/recharge analysis.

Possible consulting workstreams are: agree the project and contractual baseline; compare feasible operating options and economics; align operators, customers, grid and public authorities; and implement a measured pilot. These are this analysis's proposals, informed by BCG's public perspective [S01], not claims about BCG's involvement in any case.

The national scope broadens the examples; it does not remove disclosure bias or establish market-wide conclusions. Before publication, refresh site status and source links, confirm exact project identities and test the rendered interface on desktop and mobile. All source URLs, dates, claims and gaps are supplied in the accompanying files. Nothing has been published or sent externally.
